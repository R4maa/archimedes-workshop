SPECTRA / FIELD NOTES

Light takes
the interesting path.

This is a two-dimensional optical bench. Each beam is a bundle of 25 wavelengths, traced separately through every glass surface. Move a prism and the rainbow moves because the paths have changed.

Three rules, many possibilities

Reflection. The angle in equals the angle out. Mirrors return 96% of the incident light.

Refraction. Snell’s law, n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂, bends light at a boundary. Our glass uses an illustrative Cauchy law: n = 1.50 + 0.012D / λ², with λ in micrometres and D set by the dispersion slider.

Total internal reflection. From glass toward air, a ray beyond the critical angle cannot escape. All its energy reflects back inside. Try rotating the source in the fourth experiment.

A few honest approximations

The lenses are circular arcs sampled into 80 straight edges; real lenses are smooth. Colours and glow are designed for a screen, not a spectrometer. There is no diffraction, interference, absorption, or out-of-plane light. Overlapping glass behaves as a single material. Weak branches below 1.2% and paths beyond 18 encounters are stopped.

“Faint reflections” traces the reflected part of each glass encounter using unpolarised Fresnel reflectance. With it off, that part is discarded to keep the main path legible.

Make it your bench
